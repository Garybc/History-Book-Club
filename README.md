The History Book Card Catalog
Created by Gary Berg-Cross, July 2026 
A quick guide for reading group members
This History Book Card Catalog is a (I hope) simple shared tool for ourhistory reading group uses to select and decide on what to read next. Anyone in the group can suggest a book, everyone can rate how much they'd like to read it, and the group can see the results update live — no spreadsheets, no separate polls, no signing up for anything. It may take a few moments to learn the process but hopefully everybody can master it and provide feedback in case things need to be improved such as simplifications. 
This 1st edition of a guide walks through the whole process using one real example: adding something we haven't read but may have read about) SPQR: A History of Ancient Rome by Mary Beard, rating it, and seeing how it ranks against other suggestions.
1. Opening the app
You'll receive a link from Gary who set up your group (one of his github.io addresses – it is https://garybc.github.io/History-Book-Club/book_club_selector-good(3).html). Click it — it opens directly in your web browser. There's nothing to download and nothing to install.
The first thing you'll see is a screen asking you to choose or create a reading group – I set up one for the history group but others can be set up too:

Figure 1 — The opening screen. If your group already exists, click its name. If you're starting a brand-new group, type a name and click Create.
The history group ( Potomac History Club Group ) should already listed, just click its name and skip to Section 2. If you're the first person setting up a new group, type a clear, specific name (like "Rockville History Book Club" rather than just "History Book Club") so it doesn't get confused with someone else's group later.
2. Telling the app who you are
Once you're inside a group, you'll see a field labeled "Reader of Record" near the top. Type your name there like Gary has done to load the current list.
    • You'll need to do this every time you open the app — your name isn't saved between visits.
    • This is how the app knows whose rating belongs to whom, and who submitted which book.
    • There's no password and no account — just type your name.
3. Adding a book
Click the "Submit Books" tab if it isn't already selected. You'll see a form with a few fields. Here's what each one means:
    • Title and Author — required for Title; Author is optional but very helpful.
    • Link — a Goodreads or bookstore link, so others can look the book up. Optional.
    • Theme — a short phrase capturing what the book is about.
    • Pages — a rough page count, so people know the size of the commitment.
    • Notes — a few sentences on why it's a good pick, or what to expect. This is the most useful field for helping others decide, since most voters won't have read the book yet.

Click "File Card" when you're done. The book immediately appears on the shelf below the form, where everyone in the group can see it:

Figure 3 — The book now appears on the shelf, along with its theme, notes, page estimate, and a link.
Two buttons appear on every book card:
    • "Auto-fill details (AI)" — if the person who set up the group has enabled this feature, it can automatically look up a real link, theme, notes, and page estimate for you. If it isn't enabled, it'll say so plainly.  To me this is a big feature of using AI  and will help us understand the book candidates 
    • "Remove" — deletes the book (and any votes on it). Use this for duplicates or mistakes; there's a confirmation step so you can't remove something by accident. The idea here is that it will cull down our list over time.
4. Rating a book (this is how you vote)
Click the "Rate & Vote" tab. Every book that's been submitted shows up here as its own card (Hence the name card catalog developed by the AI).
You won't have read most of these books yet — that's expected. Instead of rating things like difficulty or length (which you can already see from the Notes and Pages on each card), you're asked a single, simple question:
"Desirability — how much do you want to read this?" on a scale of 1 (not interested) to 10 (very interested).
Drag the slider to reflect your honest interest, check off any content flags you're aware of (optional), and click "Save Rating"  (This is different than the numbers we might have used before but perhaps improving since you visually see the ratings):

Figure 4 — Rating SPQR a 9 out of 10 on the desirability slider.
    • You can rate every book on the shelf, not just one. But of course you don't necessarily have to rate everyone either and they will just have a zero score.
    • If you change your mind, just come back, move the slider, and click Save again — it updates your existing rating rather than creating a duplicate.
    • Your rating is exactly your vote. There's no separate voting step.
5. Seeing the results
Click the "Results" tab at any time to see how every book is doing. For our example, three group members (Gary, Maria, and Tom) rated SPQR at 9, 8, and 7:

Figure 5 — Results for SPQR: 3 votes, a total score of 24, and an average score of 8.0.
Each book shows four numbers:
    • Votes — how many people have rated it so far.
    • Total score — the sum of everyone's individual scores.
    • Avg score — the total divided by the number of votes. This is the fairest single number for comparing books with different vote counts.
    • Weighted rank score — used only to sort the list; see below.
At the top of the Results tab, two sliders let anyone in the group adjust how the ranking is calculated:
    • Weight: Average Score — how much a book's average rating matters.
    • Weight: Popularity (vote count) — how much the number of votes matters.
If our group prefers "the book with the highest average interest wins," push Popularity down. If your group prefers "the book most people are excited about wins, even if a couple of people rated it lower," push Popularity up. There's no right answer — try both and see which produces a pick the group agrees with.
Frequently asked questions
Do I need a Google or Claude account?
No. You just need the link. Nothing to sign up for, nothing to install.  The file lives on Gary's Github and of course can be updated and improved on if we need to  
Can I change a rating after I've saved it?
Yes — go back to the Rate & Vote tab, adjust the slider, and click Save again. It replaces your previous rating.
What if I submit a book by mistake?
Click "Remove" on that book's card. This deletes the book and any ratings on it. There's a confirmation prompt first.
Why can't I rate length or difficulty?
Since nobody's read the book yet, we only ask what you can honestly judge in advance: how interested you are. Estimated page count is shown for you automatically, so you can still factor in length when deciding your score.
Someone else's name shows up as "filed by" on a book I like — can I still vote on it?
Yes — anyone can rate any book on the shelf, regardless of who added it.

Good luck and let's see if this works well enough to be used going into the future.
